Series premise — An NFL team without a home game yet

The opening image is not a touchdown. It is an unfinished transition: an organization calling itself the Oklahoma City Bison while preparing for a season that has not begun. The cameras follow the front office, coaches and players as they attempt to turn a year of transactions into a team.

Mina Kimes serves as the fictional in-universe documentary host. The series is planned as season-long access, not a completed set of aired episodes. This edition provides an extended series treatment and opening-chapter narrative with preseason outcomes now confirmed, but without inventing unreleased interviews or regular-season results.

Act One — The offseason that refused to wait

Four marquee free-agent additions change the conversation. Yaya Diaby arrives to attack the edge. Parker Washington expands the receiving corps. Leonard 'Big Cat' Williams reunites with former Seattle teammate Bobby Wagner and gives the defensive line a veteran personality. Ikem Ekwonu adds another starting-caliber tackle.

Then the draft: Jeremiah Smith at sixth overall, Trevor Goosby at fourteenth and Ashton Hampton at nineteenth. The first round alone makes the Bison difficult to ignore. It also leaves a trail of traded picks and players that Scot McCloughan cannot simply wish away.

The editorial tension is built into the organization. Pete Carroll's 'ALWAYS COMPETE' energy meets Scot's skepticism, Sarah's logistical realism and Shane Steichen's need for a stable practice plan. Bobby Wagner must help make all of those voices work together.

Act Two — Will Howard's second test

Howard's first test was rescuing an 0–7 team. His second is less cinematic and arguably harder: preparing to be a full-season starter after the roster changes around him.

The offense now features Tetairoa McMillan, Parker Washington, Jeremiah Smith, Nyck Harbor and Tyler Warren. The talent is obvious. Chemistry is not automatic. Howard has already been candid about losing Tory Horton and Andrei Iosivas, and he has received approval for a players-only throwing session before camp. At the snapshot recorded here, that session has not yet happened.

An episode can return to that moment once the user plays it out. The archive will not pretend it has already occurred.

Act Three — A defense with a ceiling

The defense has star power at multiple levels: Diaby up front, Williams inside, Sauce Gardner and Cam Taylor-Britt in the secondary, and rookie Ashton Hampton joining the cornerback group.

Preseason Week 1 begins with Sauce Gardner dealing with a quad strain and Kapena Gushiken an ACL sprain. Madden lists each as missing one game and expected back in Preseason Week 2; neither is on injured reserve. The injuries are a reminder that even the strongest-looking roster exists in a league where depth matters.

The Bison enter this snapshot rated 84 overall, 85 on offense and 83 on defense. Those are starting coordinates, not a prediction of what the team will become.

Act Four — The stadium and the seat

The stadium is part of the narrative before its gates open. The franchise's story name for the venue is Love's Field, though the Madden interface displays Bison Field. The first preseason home dates are planned for Oklahoma's stadium, with Love's Field opening in Week 2.

Inside the larger public spectacle sits a deliberately personal detail: Patricia's Seat, a tribute to Bryan's late mother. The documentary can explain the memorial's significance without invading the family's privacy or treating grief as content.

The production boundaries are explicit. No filming inside Bryan and Q's home. Braylee's exposure is limited. The film can be intimate without assuming unlimited access.

Act Five — The story still unwritten

At Preseason Week 1, the roster screen shows 79 players against a 75-player limit. Four cuts remain pending. Cap space stands at $97.8 million. The football staff has work to do before any opening kickoff.

The documentary's central question is therefore still open: Can an organization assembled at extraordinary speed become a coherent contender? The series will be updated with actual game results, roster decisions and interviews only after they occur in the roleplay.

Closing production slate: IN PROGRESS. Three preseason wins are now confirmed; no regular-season or postseason outcomes are claimed.

Act Six — The preseason sweep

The opening on-field chapter has its results: Oklahoma City beats Seattle 31–10, San Francisco 42–7, and Los Angeles 21–0. Three wins, 94 points scored, 17 allowed, and a plus-77 differential. The franchise that entered the summer as an intriguing experiment leaves it with a target on its back.

The Madden season preview projects the Bison as AFC South winners, the AFC No. 1 seed, and Super Bowl LXII champions over Detroit. These are preseason forecasts, not completed regular-season or postseason events. The camera has not yet witnessed the Bison play a regular-season snap.

The documentary now has a central dramatic question: how does a relocated team handle championship expectations before its first official season has even begun?
