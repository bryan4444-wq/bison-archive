BUILT, NOT GIVEN

Episode 01: The First Roar

OKLAHOMA CITY BISONS • 2027 • 23:48

Private executive screening at Love’s Field — Friday, 7:12 PM, four days after the inaugural victory over Tennessee. Fictional documentary screenplay / episode transcript.

Before the screening — A family movie night

The executive screening theater at Love’s Field is usually reserved for film study, investor presentations, and the occasional private event. Tonight it’s a full-blown family movie night: forty-two oversized leather recliners, a massive 4K projection screen, Bison-blue ambient lighting, fresh popcorn, Vietnamese snacks, candy, and enough food to feed an offensive line.

Almost every important person in the organization is here.

Q enters carrying Braylee, dressed in a tiny Bison onesie. Lindy follows with the diaper bag; Q’s family gathers near the center seats.

Q: “Anh, why does your stadium have a nicer movie theater than an actual movie theater? You know we could just watch Netflix here, right?”

Lindy: “Please don’t give him another reason to come to work on his days off.”

Q’s mom: “Trời ơi, đẹp quá! This is all for watching football?”

Q: “Tonight, yes. Normally, I think they watch football players make mistakes.”

Scot McCloughan, from the back: “THAT’S EXACTLY WHAT WE DO!”

Bobby Wagner has a center-row recliner. Pete Carroll is already talking about Jacksonville. Shane Steichen has a notebook despite Sarah’s instructions.

Sarah: “Shane. Put the notebook away.”

Shane: “I’m not taking notes.”

Sarah: “You’re holding a pen.”

Shane: “It’s a habit.”

Pete: “LET THE MAN COMPETE, SARAH!”

Scot enters with a giant bucket of popcorn.

Scot: “If this documentary makes us look like a bunch of idiots, I’m retiring.”

Bobby: “You said that when Bryan hired Marshawn.”

Scot: “Still considering it.”

Will Howard sits beside Jadarian Price.

Will: “You nervous?”

JP: “About what?”

Will: “Watching yourself fumble on a screen the size of a damn house.”

JP: “Man, fuck you.”

Will steals JP’s popcorn.

JP: “GET YOUR OWN!”

Will: “Protect the popcorn, bro.”

JP: “I hate everybody on this team.”

7:19 PM — Andy has an update

Andy enters on the phone, negotiating the attempted sentimental buyback of Bryan’s former BMW M235i. The current owner knows somebody is interested but questions the above-market offer. Andy has told the dealer it’s a sentimental buyback without naming Bryan. The FK8 trade-in trail has been confirmed, but the current owner has not responded. Neither car has been purchased; no offer has been accepted.

Andy: “I’m working on it. And before you ask, no, I haven’t spent your seventy-five grand yet.”

Scot: “SEVENTY-FIVE THOUSAND FOR TWO USED CARS?!”

Andy: “SCOT, EAT YOUR POPCORN!”

7:24 PM — Beast Mode arrives

The doors swing open. Marshawn Lynch enters in a Bison hoodie, carrying two enormous bags of Skittles and an enormous drink.

Marshawn: “AYYYYYYYYY! WHERE MY MOVIE STARS AT?!”

JP: “Oh, hell no.”

Marshawn: “THERE HE IS! MR. BUTTERFINGERS!”

Will: “HE’S BEEN WAITING FOR YOU!”

Marshawn: “You gon’ hold onto the ball?”

Marshawn crouches to greet Braylee.

Marshawn: “What’s good, little Banh Mi? Damn. She already judgin’ me.”

Q: “She gets that from her dad.”

Lindy: “Absolutely.”

Marshawn calls across the room to JP.

Marshawn: “AYO, JP! YOU KNOW IF YOU DON’T FUMBLE THE REST OF THIS REGULAR SEASON, UNCLE BEAST GETS A LAMBO?”

JP: “YES! YOU’VE TOLD ME EVERY DAY!”

Continuity note: The standing Lamborghini Urus agreement was originally for zero fumbles across the entire regular season. JP already recorded two in Week 1; the documentary captures Marshawn’s teasing line, not a formal renegotiation of that agreement.

7:29 PM — Mina introduces the film

Mina Kimes steps forward beside the production director. Sarah works the control station.

Mina: “What you’re about to watch is the prerelease cut of Episode One of Built, Not Given.”

Mina: “This is not a promotional video. It’s not a highlight reel. It’s our attempt to document what it actually takes to build an NFL franchise. You’ll see the excitement. You’ll see mistakes. You’ll see disagreements. And you’ll see some very personal moments.”

She thanks Bryan for trusting the production with Patricia’s story.

Mina: “We haven’t softened the football coverage, and we haven’t manufactured anything. Everything you’re about to see happened.”

Scot: “Unfortunately.”

Mina: “And yes, Scot, you’re in it.”

Scot: “FUCK.”

Mina reminds the audience not to post clips ahead of the public premiere. Marshawn raises his hand.

Marshawn: “Am I handsome in it?”

Mina: “Unbelievably.”

Marshawn: “THAT’S WHAT I’M TALKIN’ ABOUT!”

Mina: “Alright. Let’s roll.”

00:00 — Cold open: Patricia’s seat

The theater goes dark. Onscreen, a single spotlight illuminates an empty stadium seat. Ornate metalwork. Carefully engraved lettering:

PATRICIA LUONG

Bryan’s recorded interview begins.

“It felt like my mom has a spot to watch her son’s team play at home...”

The camera pulls back to reveal the enormous empty stadium. Black screen.

“My daughter Braylee can sit near her seat, and it’s like she’s watching with Grandma.”

Q squeezes Bryan’s hand. Somewhere behind them, Pete Carroll quietly exhales.

02:10 — The Colts and the sale

ON SCREEN — JULY 2026. Archival footage of Indianapolis: Lucas Oil Stadium, the Colts horseshoe, players running through the tunnel, Peyton Manning, the 2006 championship celebration, Andrew Luck, and generations of fans.

Mina Kimes (voiceover): “For more than four decades, the Indianapolis Colts were one of the NFL’s most recognizable franchises. But in the middle of the 2026 season, the NFL received news that would change the league’s geography.”

BREAKING • 2026 — BRYAN LUONG ACQUIRES INDIANAPOLIS COLTS. New ownership signals potential relocation after the season.

Mina: “Bryan Luong didn’t buy an expansion franchise. He purchased an existing NFL organization midway through the 2026 season, inheriting its players, contracts, history, and expectations.”

Indianapolis fans react with anger, grief, and disbelief.

Indianapolis fan: “That’s our team, man. You can’t just move forty years of memories somewhere else.”

Mina: “For Indianapolis, the move meant losing a piece of the city’s identity. For Oklahoma City, it meant something the city had never experienced before: an NFL franchise of its own.”

05:00 — The relocation

Construction crews. Seats being installed. Bison-blue uniforms arriving in shipping containers. New signage. The Colts horseshoe is removed from a wall; the Bison logo takes its place.

Mina: “The relocation would take effect for the 2027 season. That gave the new ownership group less than a year to establish a new identity, prepare a stadium, build an organization, and convince an entire roster that Oklahoma City could become home.”

Bobby Wagner, interview: “The first thing I told Bryan was, you can’t just change the logo and expect everything to work. You gotta build a culture.”

Scot McCloughan, interview: “Everybody wants to talk about uniforms and stadiums. I’m looking at the roster. Who can actually play? Who fits? Who needs to go?”

Pete Carroll, interview: “You know what I saw? OPPORTUNITY! A whole new city, a whole new challenge. That’s what competing is all about!”

Scot, in the theater: “Why am I always followed by the loudest man alive?”

Pete: “BECAUSE YOU NEED MORE ENERGY!”

Scouting meetings, trades, player arrivals, offseason workouts. Will Howard’s preseason interview plays.

Will Howard: “You feel the responsibility. You’re not just trying to win games. You’re trying to establish what this team is going to be.”

JP, in the theater: “Look at Hollywood over here.”

Will: “Shut up. I’m inspirational.”

08:15 — Oklahoma City embraces its team

Downtown Oklahoma City. Families in jerseys. Flags in shop windows. Kids playing football. Fans lining up at Love’s Field.

Mina: “For a city already passionate about the Thunder, professional sports were nothing new. But the NFL brought a different kind of anticipation.”

Older fan: “I’ve watched football my whole life. Never thought I’d be able to take my grandkids to an NFL game right here in Oklahoma City.”

Another fan: “I don’t care if we go 2–15. I’m buying season tickets.”

Shane, in the theater: “Please don’t put that in the promotional material.”

Bobby: “Too late.”

The music softens. Love’s Field at sunrise.

Mina: “But beneath the excitement, the franchise’s new owner had a deeply personal reason to make its first home game unforgettable.”

11:00 — Patricia

The camera returns to Section 200. Craftsmen polish the memorial seat and its engraving. Photographs from Patricia’s life accompany Bryan’s recorded interview.

Bryan: “A hard-working woman with an immense love for her family.”

Bryan: “She worked as a dental assistant six days a week. Commuted from Renton to Olympia three days a week for school...”

Bryan: “Frankly, her work ethic is why I’m here today.”

He recalls a middle-school football game.

Bryan: “I remember getting thrown into fullback randomly and scoring a goal-line touchdown, and her cheering was the only voice I heard.”

The unveiling: thousands stand. The cover comes off the seat. Patricia’s name catches the stadium lights. The soundtrack drops out, leaving only the crowd’s applause.

Bryan: “My daughter Braylee can sit near her seat, and it’s like she’s watching with Grandma.”

Q’s mother covers her mouth. Q wipes beneath one eye.

Bryan: “That she would’ve loved her very, very much. That’s the core of what I’d want to convey to her.”

Bryan: “Grandma may not be here, but hopefully through the love that I had for her, she’d understand the love that Grandma would’ve given to her.”

The camera holds on Bryan nearly tearing up. No music, no narration. A few seconds of silence. Then the crowd begins to roar.

15:00 — Kickoff

A packed Love’s Field.

Stadium announcer: “LADIES AND GENTLEMEN... YOUR OKLAHOMA CITY BISONS!”

The team bursts through the tunnel. Will Howard leads the offense. Early drives, defensive stops, offensive adjustments.

Mina: “The Bison had spent months preparing for this moment. But football doesn’t care how perfect your opening ceremony was.”

Jadarian Price takes a handoff, finds daylight, gains seven yards—then the ball comes loose.

Commentator: “BALL’S OUT!”

Freeze frame. Shane on the sideline.

Shane: “You gotta be kidding me.”

JP: “Fuck.”

Marshawn, in the theater: “THERE IT IS!”

JP: “BRO, WE ALL SAW IT!”

Marshawn: “LOOK AT THAT BALL! IT’S LONELY!”

Will: “It’s lonely is CRAZY.”

Tennessee capitalizes on the mistake. The stadium energy shifts.

Mina: “A promising drive becomes a turnover. And suddenly, the home team is fighting to regain control.”

Bobby, interview: “Everybody makes mistakes. The question is what happens next.”

The defense answers with a stop. Howard drives the offense downfield. Another JP fumble. More adjustments. The defense repeatedly holds Tennessee in check.

Shane: “We’re not giving this game away. Execute. Finish your blocks. Protect the football.”

The soundtrack swells.

WEEK 1 • FINAL — OKLAHOMA CITY BISON 21, TENNESSEE TITANS 14.

The stadium erupts. Players embrace. Pete Carroll jumps around the sideline. The camera returns to Patricia’s seat amid the celebration.

Mina: “One game. One victory. And an entire city beginning a new chapter.”

The screen fades to black. Everyone thinks the episode has ended.

23:00 — The final hook

Black screen. A football bounces against concrete. Once. Twice.

An empty practice facility. JP sits alone in a film room watching his fumbles. A deep voice comes from the doorway.

Unknown voice: “Man... You gotta protect that damn rock.”

A silhouette appears, face obscured.

Voice: “You got talent, young blood. But talent ain’t enough.”

Cut to black.

A BEAST IS COMING TO OKC.

TO BE CONTINUED.

Marshawn Lynch (voice): “THE BEAST AIN’T HERE TO PLAY WITH Y’ALL.”

The credits roll.
