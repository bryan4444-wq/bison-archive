THE BISON TRIBUNE · DANIEL MERCER · WEEK 2, 2027

Jacksonville Wasn't a Quarterback Showcase. It Was a Warning.

Oklahoma City 31, Jacksonville 14. The Bison are 2–0, and their most convincing argument yet came without asking their quarterback to do much.

JACKSONVILLE — It would have been easy to assume the Oklahoma City Bison would follow their emotional inaugural victory with a letdown. Week 1 had everything a new franchise could ask for: a packed stadium, a tribute to Patricia Luong, and a win over Tennessee. Week 2 had none of the ceremony. Just a road game, a division opponent, and the question of whether this team could repeat the result without the occasion carrying it.

The answer was 31–14. And it was emphatic.

Jadarian Price carried 23 times for 191 yards and two touchdowns. Read that again. He averaged 8.3 yards per carry. This is the same back whose two fumbles dominated the conversation after the Tennessee game. In Jacksonville, Price didn't put the ball on the ground once. Marshawn Lynch's monthlong ball-security crusade may be louder than it needs to be, but for one Sunday the results spoke louder.

That does not retroactively erase the Week 1 fumbles or revive the extraordinary Lamborghini wager, which required zero fumbles across the entire regular season. It does, however, make the more important point: Price can be a franchise-changing runner if he protects the football.

Four carries. Two touchdowns.

Then there's Trey Benson. Four rushing attempts, 43 yards, two touchdowns. It's an almost absurd stat line. Benson did not need volume to make himself indispensable. Between him and Price, Oklahoma City got four rushing scores, and the Bison finished with 267 yards on the ground.

That production changes how we should evaluate Will Howard's afternoon. His 11-of-19 passing for 144 yards, no touchdowns and no interceptions won't lead the national highlight packages. It didn't have to. Howard made the throws the game required and avoided giving Jacksonville an extra possession. For a quarterback still proving he can run this team over a full season, knowing when not to force the issue is part of the job.

Tyler Warren led Oklahoma City with four catches for 57 yards. The rest of the receiving work was spread thin. This was not a referendum on the passing game's ceiling; it was a demonstration that the Bison have another way to win.

One injury, one opportunity

The other story deserves just as much attention. CJ Allen is on injured reserve for five weeks, and DeMarvion Overshown is taking the opportunity personally. Overshown recorded 11 tackles, 10 solo, to lead Oklahoma City. Camryn Bynum added 10. Leonard Williams contributed seven tackles and two tackles for loss.

Overshown's path here was not straightforward. Injuries hurt his free-agent market, and Oklahoma City gave him a chance when other clubs had reason to hesitate. Two weeks into the season, he is making it difficult to argue he belongs anywhere but on the field. One productive afternoon does not settle a long-term depth-chart debate, but it absolutely earns the next conversation.

What the box score doesn't settle

Jacksonville still moved the ball through the air. Tory Horton caught seven passes, although they produced only 33 yards. Jakobi Meyers finished with 76, and Brian Thomas Jr. added 64. Andrei Iosivas managed just one reception. Oklahoma City made Jacksonville work for its completions, even as the visitors' defense had to spend plenty of time tackling.

The Bison are now 2–0, both wins coming against AFC South opponents. That's an excellent beginning, not a division title. The challenge will be maintaining this physical identity when an opponent actually takes away the run.

For now, here's the most uncomfortable thought for the rest of the division: Oklahoma City just won by 17 points while asking Will Howard to throw 19 passes.

If the Bison can win like this, their opponents have more than one problem to solve.
