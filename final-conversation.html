Full written documentary

Opening — A farewell without a clean answer

The camera remains fixed on a quiet interview setting. Daniel Mercer is not there to celebrate a successful relocation. His role as an independent reporter is to ask what the franchise owes to the place it is leaving.

The special runs as a 58-minute in-universe farewell interview. This expanded archive edition reconstructs its subjects in narrative form; it does not invent verbatim responses for Bryan Luong, whose words and decisions belong to the player.

Mercer's central question hangs over the conversation: Can an owner be sincere in his gratitude to Indianapolis and still be responsible for taking the team away?

The decision and its cost

The move to Oklahoma City is explained through the larger project: a new ownership vision, a new stadium and a new regional identity. Yet Mercer returns repeatedly to the human scale. For fans, relocation is not a spreadsheet exercise. It means losing the place where family memories accumulated.

Six months of severance for affected employees and farewell packages for supporters are documented as parts of the response. Mercer treats those actions as meaningful but not exculpatory. There is no transaction that converts decades of loyalty into a settled balance.

The camera's silence is part of the story. Some questions do not have answers that sound good on television.

Taylor, Nelson and the football reset

The conversation turns to the roster. Jonathan Taylor and Quenton Nelson were not anonymous salary-cap entries. Trading them to Seattle announced that the incoming regime was willing to break with the team's most recognizable present-day identity.

That choice eventually produces a curious football footnote: Seattle wins fictional Super Bowl LXI over Buffalo, 24–13, with Taylor and Nelson. Indianapolis, meanwhile, ends 8–9 and misses the playoffs. The comparison is painful and fair. It also remains incomplete, because the draft capital and young players acquired by the Bison have not yet finished writing their own story.

Mercer presses the distinction between an intelligent long-term plan and a plan that is guaranteed to work. The documentary offers no shortcut to the latter.

The 0–7 start and Will Howard's answer

Seven straight losses leave the franchise vulnerable to every accusation made against its ownership. The change to Will Howard alters the tone. Howard goes 8–2 as the starter, with 2,403 passing yards, 14 touchdowns and five interceptions.

Mercer asks what that run means. The responsible answer is narrower than the fan's dream: it earns Howard the opportunity to lead the inaugural Bison season. It does not prove that he can carry a 17-game campaign, adapt to new receivers and survive the scrutiny that follows a breakout.

That unresolved question is what makes the new season interesting.

Seattle, the SuperSonics, and the irony

Bryan's Seattle roots make the comparison unavoidable. Seattle once lost the SuperSonics to Oklahoma City; now an owner shaped by the Pacific Northwest is bringing an NFL franchise to the same market. Mercer raises the parallel because it exposes the difference between understanding relocation as an idea and experiencing it as a loss.

The documentary does not pretend the two stories are identical. Their circumstances differ. Their emotional resonance does not. A city celebrating an arrival can still recognize what departure feels like.

That tension remains in the film rather than being smoothed into a branding slogan.

Closing — Thank you, Indianapolis

The special concludes without asking Indianapolis to forgive the move or Oklahoma City to apologize for being excited. It leaves the audience with the record of a difficult choice, the people affected and the uncertain future of the franchise.

The established closing card reads: “Thank you, Indianapolis. 1984–2026.”

Editorial note: The original interview's exact spoken lines have not been recovered. This is a full written reconstruction of the documented topics, not a fabricated transcript attributed to Bryan or Mercer.
